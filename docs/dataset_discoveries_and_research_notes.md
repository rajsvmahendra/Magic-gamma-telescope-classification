## 1. Dataset Overview & Class Distribution

### Source
- **Dataset:** MAGIC Gamma Telescope Data 2004 (UCI ML Repository)
- **Generated by:** CORSIKA Monte Carlo simulation of Cherenkov showers (not real telescope observations)
- **Total records:** 19,020
- **Features:** 10 continuous Hillas parameters + 1 class label
- **Missing values:** None

### Class Distribution

| Class | Count | Percentage |
|:--|--:|--:|
| **Gamma (signal)** `g` | 12,332 | **64.84%** |
| **Hadron (background)** `h` | 6,688 | **35.16%** |
| **Total** | **19,020** | 100% |

**Imbalance ratio:** 1.844 : 1 (Gamma to Hadron)

**Majority-class baseline accuracy:** Any classifier that simply labels every event as Gamma would score **64.84%** accuracy — without learning anything. This is the floor below which a model is useless.

> **Important caveat from the dataset documentation:** The number of Hadron events is *underestimated* in this dataset. In real MAGIC telescope data, hadronic cosmic rays outnumber gamma events by several orders of magnitude. The 65/35 split is an artefact of the simulation design. This should be prominently disclosed on the website.

## 2. Feature-by-Feature Observations

All statistics computed from the full 19,020-record dataset using Python `statistics` module.

### 2.1 fLength — Major Axis Length (mm)

| Statistic | Gamma | Hadron |
|:--|--:|--:|
| Mean | 43.65 mm | 70.94 mm |
| Median | 34.56 mm | 47.91 mm |
| Std Dev | 26.17 mm | 57.95 mm |
| Q1 | 23.67 mm | 26.14 mm |
| Q3 | 58.98 mm | 104.06 mm |
| Min | 12.19 mm | 4.28 mm |
| Max | 272.06 mm | 334.18 mm |

**Observation:** Hadron showers are on average **62.6% longer** than Gamma showers (mean 70.9 vs 43.7 mm), and their spread is *more than twice as large* (std 57.95 vs 26.17 mm). The hadron distribution has a very long right tail — some hadronic cascades span more than 334 mm on the camera plane. Gamma events are compact by comparison.

**Class separation score:** 0.607 (second highest after fAlpha) — a **strong discriminator**.

**Counterintuitive finding:** The *minimum* fLength for hadrons (4.28 mm) is *smaller* than the minimum for gammas (12.19 mm). The smallest showers on the camera can be hadrons, not gammas. Only by looking at other features (especially fAlpha) can you tell them apart.

### 2.2 fWidth — Minor Axis Width (mm)

| Statistic | Gamma | Hadron |
|:--|--:|--:|
| Mean | 18.59 mm | 28.80 mm |
| Median | 17.05 mm | 17.38 mm |
| Std Dev | 9.03 mm | 27.19 mm |
| Q1 | 12.24 mm | 11.24 mm |
| Q3 | 22.66 mm | 36.47 mm |
| Min | 0.00 mm | 0.00 mm |
| Max | 176.34 mm | 256.38 mm |

**Observation:** The *medians* of fWidth are remarkably similar (17.05 vs 17.38 mm) even though the *means* differ substantially (18.59 vs 28.80 mm). This shows that the hadron distribution is heavily right-skewed, with rare but extreme wide showers pulling the mean upward. A naïve comparison of means would overstate how different the classes are on this feature.

**Class separation score:** 0.504 — a **moderate discriminator**.

**Notable:** Both classes can produce fWidth = 0 mm (a degenerate shower collapsing to a line, occurring when only one pixel row is illuminated). Gamma event Row 1714 has fWidth = 0.000 mm exactly.

### 2.3 fSize — Shower Intensity (log10 photon count)

| Statistic | Gamma | Hadron |
|:--|--:|--:|
| Mean | 2.784 | 2.901 |
| Median | 2.705 | 2.798 |
| Std Dev | 0.463 | 0.481 |
| Q1 | 2.434 | 2.555 |
| Q3 | 3.056 | 3.180 |
| Min | 1.998 | 1.941 |
| Max | 5.010 | 5.323 |

**Observation:** fSize is a *log-scale* measurement. Even though the mean difference appears small (0.117), on a photon-count scale this means the average hadron event illuminates roughly **31% more photons** than the average gamma event (10^2.901 / 10^2.784 approx 1.31). The ranges overlap substantially.

**IQR overlap region: [2.555, 3.056]** — the central halves of both distributions sit on top of each other. fSize alone cannot reliably distinguish the classes.

**Class separation score:** 0.247 — a **weak discriminator** in isolation.

**Surprising fact:** The highest-energy event in the dataset is a *Hadron* (fSize = 5.3233), not a Gamma. Gamma events top out at fSize = 5.010.

### 2.4 fConc — Core Concentration Ratio (dimensionless)

| Statistic | Gamma | Hadron |
|:--|--:|--:|
| Mean | 0.3836 | 0.3742 |
| Median | 0.3526 | 0.3580 |
| Std Dev | 0.1780 | 0.1913 |
| Min | 0.0139 | 0.0131 |
| Max | 0.8889 | 0.8930 |

**Observation:** fConc is the fraction of total light in the two brightest pixels. The class means differ by only **0.0094** — essentially indistinguishable at first glance. The distributions heavily overlap. Despite common intuition that gamma showers should be more concentrated, the global mean concentration ratio is nearly identical.

**Class separation score: 0.051 — the second weakest discriminator.** This surprises many students who expect concentration to be a strong gamma signal.

**Why this happens:** While individual compact gamma showers do have high fConc, many gamma events are energetic enough to spread light across many pixels, lowering their concentration. High-energy gammas can look "dilute" on the camera.

### 2.5 fConc1 — Peak Concentration Ratio (dimensionless)

| Statistic | Gamma | Hadron |
|:--|--:|--:|
| Mean | 0.2150 | 0.2139 |
| Median | 0.1950 | 0.2002 |
| Std Dev | 0.1062 | 0.1181 |

**Observation:** fConc1 (ratio of the single brightest pixel) is the **weakest discriminator of all ten features** (separation score: 0.010). Gamma and Hadron distributions are virtually identical at this level. fConc1 is tightly correlated with fConc (Pearson r = 0.979 across all events), so they carry redundant information.

**Key takeaway for the website:** A model that only uses the two concentration features would barely outperform random guessing.

### 2.6 fAsym — Longitudinal Asymmetry (mm)

| Statistic | Gamma | Hadron |
|:--|--:|--:|
| Mean | +3.24 mm | −18.29 mm |
| Median | +6.64 mm | −1.54 mm |
| Std Dev | 39.63 mm | 82.30 mm |
| Min | −349.76 mm | −457.92 mm |
| Max | +219.90 mm | +575.24 mm |

**Observation:** fAsym measures the signed displacement from the brightest pixel to the shower center, projected along the major axis. The means differ by ~21 mm, but the spreads are enormous. The hadron asymmetry standard deviation is **2.08 times larger** than for gammas — hadronic showers are far more erratic in their charge distribution. The extreme values tell dramatic stories: one hadron event (Row 12338 in the data) has fAsym = +298.6 mm — the energy deposition peak is nearly a third of a meter from the shower center.

**Class separation score:** 0.333 — a **moderate discriminator**.

| Min | −198.87 mm | −331.78 mm |
| Max | +215.89 mm | +238.32 mm |

**Observation:** The third moment (skewness indicator) along the major axis has notably different means: gamma showers tend to have a positive skewness (mean +17.8 mm), meaning the energy deposition leans towards one end. Hadrons show near-zero mean (−2.85 mm) but with *twice the spread* (std 70.7 vs 33.9 mm).

**Class separation score:** 0.373 — **moderate discriminator**.

### 2.9 fAlpha — Orientation Angle (degrees)

| Statistic | Gamma | Hadron |
|:--|--:|--:|
| Mean | 18.78° | 43.99° |
| Median | 9.71° | 43.38° |
| Std Dev | 21.48° | 25.98° |
| Q1 | 3.61° | 21.19° |
| Q3 | 25.81° | 66.56° |
| Min | 0.00° | 0.01° |
| Max | 90.00° | 90.00° |

**fAlpha is the single most powerful individual discriminator** (separation score: **1.057**).

**Key statistics:**
- **50.8%** of Gamma events have fAlpha < 10° — their ellipse points almost directly at the camera center (the source direction).
- Only **11.4%** of Hadron events have fAlpha < 10°.
- **69.0%** of Gamma events have fAlpha < 20°; only **23.7%** of Hadrons do.
- **31.5%** of Hadron events have fAlpha > 60°; only **8.0%** of Gammas do.
- **26.1%** of Hadron events fall inside the Gamma IQR [3.6°, 25.8°] — a significant overlap zone where the two classes are genuinely ambiguous.

**Physical explanation:** Gamma rays from a point source produce showers whose major axis points back towards the source direction (the camera center). Random cosmic rays arrive from all directions, producing showers with random orientations — their fAlpha values are approximately uniformly distributed between 0° and 90°. This is why the hadron distribution is nearly flat while the gamma distribution is sharply peaked near 0°.

**Notable:** Both classes have events reaching exactly fAlpha = 90° (6 gamma, 8 hadron). These are edge cases where the shower is perfectly perpendicular to the source direction.

### 2.10 fDist — Radial Distance (mm)

| Statistic | Gamma | Hadron |
|:--|--:|--:|
| Mean | 190.23 mm | 200.43 mm |
| Median | 188.12 mm | 199.87 mm |
| Std Dev | 70.30 mm | 81.87 mm |
| Min | 5.75 mm | 1.28 mm |
| Max | 450.40 mm | 495.56 mm |

**Observation:** The mean radial distance is similar for both classes (190 vs 200 mm), and the distributions overlap substantially. Hadron showers tend to be found slightly further from the camera center, but the difference is small and unreliable as a sole discriminator.

**Class separation score:** 0.134 — **weak discriminator**.

**Interesting:** Within gamma events only, fAlpha and fDist show a moderate negative correlation (Pearson r = −0.425): events that are further from the camera center tend to have smaller orientation angles. This makes geometric sense — extended showers at larger radii tend to point more directly at the origin.

### 3.3 The Concentration Paradox

The most counterintuitive finding: **fConc and fConc1 are the two weakest discriminators** between gamma and hadron events. Students often expect gamma showers (compact electromagnetic cascades) to be more concentrated in fewer pixels than hadronic showers (diffuse hadronic cascades). The data does not support this expectation at the global level:

- Gamma fConc mean: **0.3836**
- Hadron fConc mean: **0.3742**
- Difference: **0.0094** — nearly zero

The reason is that the concentration ratio is defined relative to fSize. High-energy gamma events create many photons spread over many pixels, producing low concentration values. The correlation of fConc with fSize (r = −0.851) confirms this.

### 3.4 The Asymmetry Scale Gap

Hadron events exhibit dramatically more variable asymmetry and third moments than gamma events:
- fAsym std ratio (Hadron/Gamma): **2.08×**
- fM3Long std ratio: **2.08×**
- fM3Trans std ratio: **2.21×**

This reflects the physically irregular nature of hadronic cascades — multiple secondary particles scatter in different directions, creating asymmetric and skewed light patterns that vary wildly from shower to shower.

### 3.5 Surprising fLength Minimum

The *smallest* fLength in the entire dataset belongs to a **Hadron** (4.28 mm), not a Gamma. The gamma minimum is 12.19 mm. Very compact, tiny showers are predominantly hadronic — they may correspond to low-energy particles that only produce a small cluster of illuminated pixels. Without fAlpha, they would be nearly impossible to distinguish from gammas.

## 6. Feature Importance & Model Findings

### 6.1 Why Random Forest Outperforms Logistic Regression

The Random Forest (RF) achieves higher accuracy, recall, and ROC-AUC across the board. Several factors explain this:

**1. Non-linear decision boundaries:**  
RF can capture complex, non-linear interactions between features (e.g., "if fAlpha is high AND fLength is short AND fConc is above 0.5, then predict hadron"). Logistic Regression can only draw a linear hyperplane in the 10-dimensional feature space, missing curved boundaries that exist in the data.

**2. Robustness to outliers:**  
Several features (fAsym, fM3Long, fLength) have extreme outliers (hadron std >> mean). RF, by splitting on ranked thresholds within individual trees, is naturally resistant to extreme values. LR, even with StandardScaler normalization, can be distorted by residual extreme observations.

**3. Feature interaction capture:**  
The fConc–fSize inverse correlation (r = −0.851) and fConc–fConc1 redundancy (r = 0.976) can degrade LR performance when both are included. RF automatically learns which combinations of features provide information at each tree node.

**4. Class imbalance tolerance:**  
The 65/35 class imbalance means a linear model biased toward the majority class will sacrifice hadron recall for gamma precision. RF with 100 trees and Gini impurity can maintain a better trade-off across the feature space.

## 7. Model Error Observations

### 7.1 Error Asymmetry Pattern

| Error Type | RF Count | LR Count | RF vs LR |
|:--|--:|--:|:--|
| False Alarms (Hadron predicted as Gamma) | 477 | 567 | RF: 15.9% fewer |
| Missed Detections (Gamma predicted as Hadron) | 119 | 228 | RF: 47.8% fewer |

The most striking asymmetry: RF commits **47.8% fewer missed gamma detections** than LR. This is the most practically important error type in astronomy (missing real signal).

### 7.2 Where Errors Most Likely Concentrate

Based on the dataset analysis, errors most likely cluster in events with:

1. **High fAlpha for Gammas (> 45°):** Only 8.0% of gammas have fAlpha > 60°, but these are the events where gamma alignment evidence is weakest. Without a clear pointing signature, models fall back on shape features which overlap more heavily. Event E (Row 5468, fAlpha = 47.6°) exemplifies this.

2. **Low fAlpha for Hadrons (< 20°):** 23.7% of hadrons fall below 20°, and 26.1% fall inside the gamma IQR for fAlpha [3.6°, 25.8°]. These are the natural false alarm producers. Event F (Row 15978, fAlpha = 19.4°) is exactly this scenario.

3. **Short, compact hadrons:** Rows with hadron fLength near or below the gamma minimum (12.19 mm) mimic the size signature of gamma events. Examples include the smallest-fLength hadron at 4.28 mm.

4. **Large, energetic gammas:** Events like Row 2645 (fSize = 5.01, nearly circular) have morphology that resembles hadronic showers and elevated fAlpha.

### 7.3 ROC-AUC Significance

The ROC-AUC score captures performance across all possible probability thresholds:
- RF AUC: **0.8869**
- LR AUC: **0.8388**
- Random classifier baseline: 0.5000

The gap of 0.0481 AUC units is meaningful in practice. At low false-alarm thresholds (the scientifically important regime where background contamination must be minimized), RF consistently dominates LR. This aligns with the dataset documentation's recommendation to use ROC curves for comparison rather than raw accuracy.
